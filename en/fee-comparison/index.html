Crypto card fees — does 0% really mean zero?

'0% fees' does not mean the real cost is zero. Costs can arise at every step money moves: top-up, crypto conversion, FX and spread, payment, foreign payment and ATM.

As of 11 October 2026

Six places costs arise

Look at what finally leaves your pocket, not the 0%.

  1. Top-upA fee may apply when loading the card (e.g. Anytap 2%).
  2. Crypto conversionConverting volatile coins creates a sell spread (e.g. KAST sells volatile coins on deposit).
  3. FX / rate appliedPaying in a currency other than the card's base adds FX fees and spread (KAST non-USD 0.5–1.75%, Ether.fi 1%).
  4. Card paymentCheck the payment currency and how the rate is handled.
  5. Foreign paymentOverseas merchants may add a fixed fee (e.g. Anytap 1% + $0.30).
  6. ATMCash withdrawals have their own rate and minimum (KAST $3 + 2%, Anytap 2% min. $1).
TOTAL COST FORMULA

Total cost = issuance + annual fee + top-up + conversion + FX fee + FX spread + foreign payment + ATM − cashback actually received.

Example: spending the equivalent of $2,000 a month in KRW on KAST Standard earns up to $30, but a 1% FX fee takes $20, leaving about $10.

CUSTOMER WARNING

A provider that does not publish its fees transparently on its official website cannot be an object of trust.

Fee disclosure is not a favour; it is the basic information users need to know the contract terms in advance. If issuance, top-up, conversion, FX spread, foreign payment and ATM fees cannot be checked item by item, you cannot calculate the total cost before signing up.

Phrases like 'free', '0%' or 'lowest in the industry' without stated rates leave open the possibility that costs are hidden in the spread or conversion step.

Three things to check first

Itemised official fee table

Are all fees listed one by one?

Advance notice of changes

Do the terms require notice before rates change?

FX reference

Is the network reference rate used and the markup stated?

VERIFY

If any of these cannot be confirmed, make a small test payment and check the actual charge before depositing a large amount. Figures are on the comparison page.
