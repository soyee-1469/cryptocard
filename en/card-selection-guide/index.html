Crypto card selection guide

Uncertain cashback, or guaranteed functionality?

Decide your criterion before comparing rates. Weighing two things of different nature on one scale makes a rational choice hard.

Cashback is a conditional benefit whose timing, payout asset and upkeep depend on the provider's policy, whereas features and limits are disclosed and fixed as contract terms.

Cashback or functionality — which should guide you?

1. Cashback is an expected value; features are fixed values

Cashback becomes certain only after passing through rate, cap, tier, payout asset and exclusions — each changeable by the provider. Limits, card-to-card transfers and external withdrawals are disclosed contract terms that work as stated.

2. Revisit why you want a crypto card

The core value of a crypto card is turning exchange or wallet assets into a payment method without a bank account. If a payment is declined for lack of limit, or your balance cannot be withdrawn, the rate means nothing.

3. Rewards come with costs and lock-ins

Large annual fees, staking own tokens or cashback usable only in-app are costs and risks in themselves. Always ask what you must pay or lock up to keep the rate.

4. Conclusion — small-spend rewards or real-use functionality

For small monthly spend aimed at rewards, a cashback card can make sense. For large payments or managing funds from your own wallet, choosing on limits and transfer features saves more in practice.

MOST IMPORTANT CHECK

A card that does not disclose its fees item by item cannot be compared, because savings cannot be calculated.

The first criterion is not the size of the rate but how fully the fee system is disclosed. Compare first the cards with an itemised official fee table, advance-notice rules for rate changes and a stated FX reference.
